**Keep South Florida's already deployed Standard tariff:** $1.00 start, $0.95/road-mile and $0.32/routed-minute, $3 fare minimum, 99% Fare for Operator, minimum $2.50 Platform Fee solved by `backend/economics.js`. ### Direct institutional evidence
- Stanford GSB, Bimpikis/Candogan/Saban, *Spatial Pricing in Ridesharing Networks* (2019): geographic supply/demand and local balance can change welfare and optimal prices. https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/spatial-pricing-ridesharing-networks
- MIT Sloan, Freund/van Ryzin, *Pricing Fast and Slow* (2025): rapidly changing dynamic prices can trigger wait-then-request oscillations and reduce operational efficiency; no endorsement of any particular fare. https://mitsloan.mit.edu/faculty/directory/daniel-freund
- Harvard Business School, Frances Frei, *The Four Things a Service Business Must Get Right* (2008): design the offering alongside a sustainable funding mechanism and the service operations that deliver it. https://hbr.org/2008/04/the-four-things-a-service-business-must-get-right
- Google, Firestore best practices/pricing: minimize billable reads and index scans, use bounded cursors, avoid duplicate listeners/hot document write churn, distinguish idle cost from Traveler-driven cost. https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/best-practices ; https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/pricing
- Amazon Builders' Library, *Making retries safe with idempotent APIs*: an identical retry reuses the same token; altered request parameters must be rejected or distinctly authorized. https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-apis/
- Google SRE, *Eliminating Toil*: reject unnecessary repetitive work and automatically execute bounded, observable maintenance. https://sre.google/workbook/eliminating-toil/
- NASA SWE-055, *Requirements Validation*: code verification/simulation alone is not customer-operating-environment validation; physical handoff and failure behavior must be demonstrated. https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16449673/SWE-055%2B-%2BRequirements%2BValidation
- GTFS Schedule reference and best practices: routes, stops, trip-specific boarding times, weekday/weekend calendars, special-day exceptions and transfers must be obtained from current official source feeds. https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/reference/ ; https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/schedule-best-practices/
- OpenTripPlanner official API: station and journey planning is not a free text lookup; its GraphQL service computes multimodal itineraries and requires running compute. https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/apis/Apis/
- Microsoft Research, *Round-Based Public Transit Routing* (2012) offers a possible simpler future timetable-routing algorithm; an academic algorithm is **not** a production replacement until implemented, integration-validated and benchmarked. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/round-based-public-transit-routing/

## Confirmed architecture, cost and quota
- Existing Render `American-Rider-Server` is a single Starter service, 0.5 vCPU / 512 MB, $7/month list price, auto-deploys `release/current`; automatic instance scaling **is not configured**. A brief ~120MB memory observation under light use is not proof of production throughput.
- The September OTP graph was measured at ~1.5GB **loaded** memory. It cannot share the current 512MB box in that form. The historical $85 4GB Render server plus ~$2.50 persistent disk is **one optional hosting quote**, not a bus data charge, university recommendation, licensed schedule subscription or required $88 purchase. Prefer benchmarking simpler methods before authorizing a recurring new service; do not falsely imply no compute is needed for complete live car→transit→car routing.
- **Free static GTFS feed acquisition and preselection are already coded.** The three bus agencies are Miami-Dade, Broward County Transit and Palm Tran; Tri-Rail and Brightline are rail feeds and independent of bus preselection. PR #20 now prepares weekday, Saturday and Sunday allow-lists from the **three** official bus source files; missing independent train timetable cannot block refreshing bus preselection. A missing required **bus** schedule fails closed without overwriting last good list.
- The September single-day list uses a busiest-stop aggregate. It cannot establish departure at an arbitrary stop on Sunday. PR #20 tests at the actual proposed boarding-stop arrival against a timetable, allowing up to **15-minute waits on weekday 07:00–19:00** and **30 minutes at other hours/weekends**. Availability still requires a valid trip in the transport planner. The three-profile table is **not deployed** until official source schedules are refreshed and the review PR is approved.
- The old Chicago hourly research writer and avoidable idle reads were addressed in earlier merged releases (PR #101/#103 and recovery improvements); reintroducing periodic city-wide polls would violate existing national cost policy. Firebase Spark's 50K reads/20K writes daily constrain startup tests, and Blaze's usage-based capacity was already approved as the scaling design but **billing has not been verified/upgraded here**. A missing live billing observation cannot be called a repaired quota or a current breach.
- In production we have not observed a live separate OTP service, a current synchronized GTFS graph, end-to-end live train/bus journeys, GPS-based stop access on real devices, actual Stripe linked Smart Travel settlement or proven production database concurrency. Preserve real insurance/screening, payment, geographic and state-law checks. Do not resurrect retired `market_admission` administrative gating from superseded ledger prose.

## Testing policy / release gates
New or changed source: execute the existing exact-SHA release checks, two exhaustive passes, 10K/100K/1M **synthetic workload** campaigns, adversarial retries, data availability, calendar and DST tests. The review branch adds independent agency schedules and no-extra-provider-call route logic. **Never** describe a test named “million” as one million simultaneous real transactions.

Production proof still required, with no real passengers or unapproved charges:
1. Refresh official current GTFS and independently verify calendar coverage and route/stop ID stability for each source; inspect required data license/terms and timetable age.
2. Integrate current graph or a rigorously equivalent routing service; verify correct stop/time, transfers, missing feeds, operating day, operator GPS availability, late service, Apple/Android travel transitions.
3. Verify revalidation at real handoff, appropriate missed-connection recovery, unknown agency fares visibly non-totaled, fare quotation consent at exact integer cents, locked one-use Smart car continuity.
4. Inspect signed test-mode payment/refund/transfer objects tied to one Travel Number; measure billed Firestore reads/writes and idle baselines after quotas reset; verify no call-storm on failure.
5. Validate physical-device and jurisdiction/legal/safety evidence separately. Passing tests alone does not authorize commercial Travel.

## Decision
**No new monthly subscription or fare change. Keep trains and practical buses.** Retain the existing planning architecture as a candidate until an objectively cheaper, fully usable router is benchmarked; a static stop table alone cannot promise a complete itinerary. PR #20 passed all seven exact-head CI checks and was merged to `release/current` at `5c354d311d56fc962f5dfa1a141ecf030da956ee`; Render backend, website and preview were verified live on that SHA. **This does not commission real GTFS feeds, the transit planner, linked payment flows or physical devices.** Use the Founder-first exception view and existing code; do not duplicate market permissions, cost collectors or payment flows.
