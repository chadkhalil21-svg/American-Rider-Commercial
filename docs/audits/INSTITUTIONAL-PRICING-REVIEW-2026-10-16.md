## Source-led findings and applications
1. **Stanford GSB, Bimpikis/Candogan/Saban, Spatial Pricing in Ridesharing Networks (2019)**: local directional balance between travel origin/destination, operator supply, and demand matters. American Rider already has one reusable national engine with a local explicit tariff registry. Keep that architecture; do not create a Florida-specific second fare engine or apply one city's rates nationwide. https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/spatial-pricing-ridesharing-networks
2. **Columbia Business School, Besbes/Castro/Lobel, Surge Pricing and Its Spatial Supply Response (2021)**: pricing affects where supply relocates, and simplistic revenue maximization can underserve regions. Do not optimize Traveler price alone. Evaluate pickup ETA, denied demand, Operator repositioning and balanced access to neighborhoods. https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/surge-pricing-and-its-spatial-supply-response
3. **Columbia, Buchholz/Doval/Kastl/Matejka/Salz, Value of Time (2020)**: demand responds to price and wait. Model booking conversion and pickup time together; a cheaper fare with no available Operator is not a functional offering. https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/value-time-evidence-auctioned-cab-rides
4. **MIT Sloan, Freund/van Ryzin, Pricing Fast and Slow (2025)**: rapidly changing dynamic prices can create strategic waiting and instability. Keep ratified per-region coefficients stable in initial service. Do not add automatic minute-by-minute surge without evidence and controlled holdout. https://mitsloan.mit.edu/faculty/directory/daniel-freund
5. **Harvard Business School, Michael Luca, Designing Online Marketplaces: Trust and Reputation Mechanisms (2016)** and Frances Frei's service-design research: high price, low price, and advertised quality must be backed by credible customer experience and a financially sustainable service. Show one Total, avoid artificial savings claims, measure reliability/recovery and Operator supply. https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-024f-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b ; https://hbr.org/2008/04/the-four-things-a-service-business-must-get-right
6. **Apple HIG + IDEO + UK Government Service Standard**: disclose a confirmed Total, retain visible route/time assumptions, avoid surprising price changes, and design recovery before charging. Prototype with actual Travelers and Operators. No need to display the internal fee equation or new pricing sliders in booking. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles/ ; https://www.ideo.com/ ; https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-4-make-the-service-simple-to-use
7. **Google Firestore pricing and SRE**: batch and cache historical price research; minimize index reads and repeated idle database work. Already present: 30-second bounded market reference cache, skip uncommissioned collectors, fingerprint duplicate observations. Avoid new per-quote external collector calls or high-frequency city-wide pricing sweeps. https://cloud.google.com/firestore/pricing ; https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/best-practices ; https://sre.google/workbook/eliminating-toil/
8. **Amazon Builders' Library + Stripe**: replay an identical booking/pricing request without a second charge; reject the same idempotency key when the amount or destination differs. Existing quote reapproval and refund/retry tests must continue to gate each pricing release. No synthetic test can certify live Stripe charge integrity. https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/ ; https://docs.stripe.com/api/errors
9. **NASA SWE-029 and SWE-067**: source/unit/synthetic work is verification, not full validation in a real operating environment. Independent signed phone tests, live routing, billing and price-consent recovery still require observation in intended conditions. https://swehb.nasa.gov/pages/viewpage.action?navigatingVersions=true&pageId=23200749 ; https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/SWEHBVB/pages/32604539/SWE-067%2B-%2BVerify%2BImplementation
10. **Consumer Reports, June 2026 observed same-route quote variation**: external prices are noisy and might include promotions; a single quote or a fake strike-through number is not a defensible ordinary market median. Require independent contemporaneous source and confidence. The City of Chicago 2026 TNC trip dataset and NYC HVFHV data provide useful *methods and sanity checks* but cannot stand in for actual Florida all-in contemporaneous quotes. https://www.consumerreports.org/money/questionable-business-practices/uber-lyft-different-prices-for-same-ride-and-fake-discounts-a1093538909/ ; https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Transportation-Network-Providers-Trips-2026-/8bbv-3wbe/data ; https://www.nyc.gov/site/tlc/about/tlc-trip-record-data.page

## Code/evidence audit
- **Established commercial price (do not change without controlled approval):** South Florida Standard $1 start + $0.95 routed-mile + $0.32 routed-minute, $3 Fare minimum, 99% to Operator, $2.50 Platform Fee minimum. One server integer-cent Traveler Total including applicable lawful pass-throughs. Quote and booking recheck agreed cents and refuse stale old client prices.
- **Defect corrected in this PR:** `docs/FARE-MODEL.md` still described the older $0.85/mile and $0.15/minute tariff, and its fee example table overstated newer domestic and foreign fee amounts. The source-of-truth check now compares the document directly against runtime register/solver. No production coefficient changes.
- **Market evidence is not yet commissioned:** Verified ordinary matched contemporaneous Florida 2026 TNC all-in quote basket is absent. `pricing-policy.js` keeps the 90% *research target*, but `market-reference-service.js` quality-gates freshness, provenance and independent sources; research-only estimates cannot automatically promote price changes. Current source records alone are not sufficient evidence for a market price reset.
- **Cost is not yet fully loaded:** `economics.js` includes processor, Connect, reserve, fixed per-Travel operations allowance and minimum modeled platform contribution. It cannot establish unquoted corporate insurance, source-verification labor, fraud loss, support, compliance or fleet acquisition cost. Do not say a positive modeled contribution equals company profit.
- **Potential Operator livelihood gap:** 99% of paid Fare is not 99% net income. A deterministic **illustration only**: 7 routed miles/18 routed minutes gives $13.41 Fare, $13.28 Operator fare earnings and $15.91 domestic Traveler Total before pass-throughs. At 50% utilization, 30% extra unpaid distance, and **76¢/mile as an IRS optional-tax-rate proxy** (not the Operator's actual vehicle cost), 36 duty minutes and 9.1 total miles imply ~$6.92 vehicle-cost proxy and ~$10.60/hour remaining before other expenses/tax. Different assumptions change this outcome radically. Obtain measured pickup, unpaid minutes, vehicle and insurance expenses before claiming sustainable take-home earnings. https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/standard-mileage-rates ; https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/taxi-drivers-and-chauffeurs.htm
- **National rate research already exists:** `national-pricing-candidate.js` and `national-regional-pricing.js` use verified local geography and explicit wage, utilization, empty-mile and vehicle cost assumptions. Keep these research-only until actual evidence, fairness studies, and production consent gates pass.

## Alternative solutions screened
- **Competitor-linked real-time algorithmic surge:** reject for launch; rights, complexity, instability, perceived unfairness, source costs and difficulty verifying signal provenance outweigh speculative gains.
- **Flat nationwide per-mile tariff:** reject; fails geographic supply, travel-time and legal economics differences.
- **Price purely at a fixed $2.50 platform fee:** reject; larger fares, foreign cards and pass-throughs can violate the modeled contribution floor.
- **Operator subscription instead of per-Travel commission:** not a transparent replacement for the settled 99% agreement; introduces charging during periods without sufficient requests and changes contracts.
- **More useful near-term path:** retain existing stable rates and evidence-promotion gates; add a finite South Florida matched ordinary-price basket, publish a research-only comparison report, collect consented first-party quote-to-completion signals at bounded cost, then run shadow-price alternatives on exactly the same synthetic routes before proposing a tariff PR.
- **Equity/robustness check:** stress nonairport, airport, short, long, peak, weekend, low-income geography, disability/accessible (only when actually serviced), protected locations, interruptions and sparse supply. Never introduce individualized fares based on personal characteristics or browsing behavior.
